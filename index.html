Experience

Thirty years in engineering and technology delivery, across R&D, product lifecycle management, global delivery and supply chain. The career began in R&D at Robert Bosch, working alongside plant procurement, plant engineering, quality and shop-floor teams to solve daily production problems from a design perspective. Engineering and delivery roles at Siemens, Mahindra Satyam and Philips followed, all within global delivery models.

At Geometric, as Client Partner, came P&L ownership for a portfolio of about USD 10M across global automotive and engineering clients, covering revenue, cost, margin and executive commercial reviews, with a team of about 200. A later role at PROLIM Solutions covered the client relationship and delivery for an enterprise PLM programme delivered globally. That was the services side of the table.

At Schneider Electric, I was the client. As Director of the India GCC from 2017 to 2024, I built the case for moving a business line's India work out of a partner-run offshore development centre, in place since 2009, and into a Global Capability Center of Schneider's own. The case was approved by the business line's SVP and CFO and by the India CEO and CFO. The build that followed covered the location and structure decisions, the first SEZ entity in Schneider India, its support functions, the partner agreements and a purpose-built site.

The centre went live in December 2020, in the middle of COVID, and grew faster than planned as global teams gained confidence in it. A site in Germany was consolidated into Hyderabad in 2021, and a second centre opened in 2023. The role reported to the US business-group VP and was the senior link between global headquarters and India. It carried accountability for the centre's operations and project governance, and for representing the entity with government agencies, auditors and clients.

From 2021 the role also carried supply and delivery accountability for Process Automation, whose products are made mainly at the Mexico factory and shipped to customers in every region. That work began alongside the GCC role, when the post-COVID component shortage pushed lead times sharply up, and became full time with a move to Boston in 2024 as Director, Supply Quality and Logistics. It meant aligning R&D, procurement, manufacturing, quality and sales around one view of what was critical, and acting as the single point of contact for delivery escalations across more than 20 suppliers and 20 customers.

Achievements

At Schneider Electric, 2017 to 2025

A zero-disruption move to an owned GCC
Relocated about 200 people and a hardware lab of about 40,000 items into a purpose-built site during COVID. It went live in December 2020 with zero disruption to live work.
Growth well beyond the plan
The centre grew from about 250 to about 600 professionals and from 3 to 5 product lines. Headcount doubled in about 2.5 years against a plan of 5% a year, leading to a second centre in 2023.
Savings beyond the business case
Accountable for savings of about 10% of the annual operating budget, over and above what the approved business case planned, from SEZ tax benefits and lower partner rates after insourcing. Set up the first SEZ entity in Schneider India.
A delivery recovery customers felt
After the post-COVID component crisis, lead times fell by around 60 to 70% from their peaks, and on-time delivery rose from under 50% to over 70%.
From execution capacity to roadmap contributor
The GCC moved on to new technologies and global product roadmaps, work the partner model had not allowed. Global teams rated projects above 4.5 out of 5 at close.
Retention through rapid growth
Employee attrition stayed at around 8 to 10% a year while the centre grew 2.4 times.
